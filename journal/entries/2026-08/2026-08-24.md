## 8:16:15 AM CDT - Commit: c51b58c

### Summary
The developer reordered Phase B of the research plan to run the practitioner workflow studies before the capability spike, reversing the original sequence. This change—documented as Decision 65—shifts from surveying what the platform provides first to identifying what practitioners actually need first, then checking whether the platform already supports those needs.

The reasoning centers on anchoring bias: reading capability documentation first establishes a fixed understanding of what's supported, which then filters how practitioners' solutions are interpreted. Ambitious approaches get quietly reclassified as unsupported or workarounds, narrowing the target invisibly. By studying Viktor's and Michael's working setups first, the developer ensures their solutions are evaluated on their own terms rather than through the lens of what the platform currently offers.

The reorder preserves the safeguard that mattered most from the old sequence: if a spike surfaces a mechanism that looks central and expensive to adopt, that specific capability question gets checked immediately rather than deferred, preventing large design decisions from resting on false assumptions about platform support.

The developer also re-scoped Milestone B1 from a general capability survey into a focused pass that answers only the specific questions the practitioner studies raise. This produces actionable answers rather than a document that ages before it gets used. Verification confirmed the reorder breaks no prerequisites—no Phase B milestone's initial steps ever depended on B1 being complete first—so the change costs nothing in unmet dependencies.

The commit includes updates to the PRD documenting this decision, along with daily and weekly summaries capturing the broader context of audit work that led to this reframing.
