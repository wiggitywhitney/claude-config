## 6:49:29 AM PDT - Commit: 6a81e2d

### Summary
The developer resolved an open research question about hook events in the Claude Code platform by examining the shipped binary at version 2.1.278. The investigation confirmed that `UserPromptSubmit` exists as a distinct hook event—separate from `UserPromptExpansion`—that is fully wired to block a prompt or attach context to it, confirmed for the CLI/interactive prompt-submission path this probe exercised (other channels weren't checked). This capability wasn't found in the earlier 2.1.221 enumeration from August 4th and is confirmed present at 2.1.278; the exact version it was introduced in, between those two, is unknown. An earlier draft of this finding also claimed the hook could rewrite prompt text, which was retracted after the supporting evidence turned out to trace to a broader code path rather than to the hook itself.

The finding closes Milestone B2's Decision 40, which had asked whether a per-prompt hook mechanism existed to support the structural rule-compliance pattern cited in a community case study (raising compliance from roughly 20% to 84%). The developer documented this discovery across four files: added a detailed entry to the progress log explaining the finding and its implications, expanded the audit decisions document with the full re-check against the 2.1.278 binary including the specific control-flow strings found, marked the corresponding question in the spike document as resolved with references to the supporting evidence, and updated the PRD to close out the open item while deferring adoption decisions to a later milestone.
