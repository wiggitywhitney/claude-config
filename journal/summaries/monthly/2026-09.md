# Monthly Summary — 2026-09

## Month in Review

The developer spent the month closing out a long-lived feature branch and wrapping up a configuration audit project. The work had two distinct phases: first, merging the branch with main to surface divergence issues and fix bugs that emerged, then settling critical open questions and cleaning up documentation for the audit.

Week 1 was dominated by the branch reconciliation. Early fixes addressed bugs in the symlink checker and card-making skill that would've silently produced incorrect output. The real discovery came mid-week when merging main revealed a systemic design problem: nearly all installed skills and the rules directory itself are symlinks into the working tree, which means branch state silently determines what loads in every session. This created a concrete example where a skill measured different token counts on different branches. The developer also found that long-lived branches combined with post-commit hooks created silent divergence git won't catch—a writing-voice rule would merge into duplicates without flagging a conflict. They fixed incorrect shell commands throughout the codebase, created new agent definitions, and documented two distinct stop states in CodeRabbit's review service that had been conflated (rate limits versus auto-pause). By week's end, they'd also discovered that upstream skills had barely changed in seven months while local copies had diverged significantly, which flipped their reconciliation strategy.

Week 2 shifted to the configuration audit project. Rather than relying on documentation, the developer conducted hands-on testing and found earlier research needed correction. The sandbox now routes network approvals through a classifier, and they traced a concrete TLS error in `gh` to a fixable certificate verification issue. They also discovered that `gcloud` and `terraform` weren't actually tested despite being documented. The major work came from settling two critical open questions: they proved the sandbox escape hatch is model-mediated (not automatic) through targeted testing, and they narrowed a six-week-old question about project-level import compaction by measuring actual behavior. They documented four new gotcha files and repaired two commands that couldn't work as written.

Week 3 wrapped up loose ends. The developer resolved a long-standing question about the `UserPromptSubmit` hook by testing the binary—it can block prompts or attach context, but can't rewrite prompt text as they'd initially claimed. They walked back that overclaim across the documentation. They also closed out Decision 94 with a fresh-session probe: project-level imports don't resolve into context, eliminating a major uncertainty in downstream calculations. They triaged CodeRabbit findings using a structured rubric and fixed five documentation issues, moving the milestone from 5 closed question groups to 7.
